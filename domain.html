Domain

Textile waste, circular economy intelligence and an integrated digital marketplace.

1. Introduction

TexCycle AI addresses the information and coordination barriers that make textile waste difficult to identify, assess, value and connect with suitable downstream users. The platform treats textile waste as structured circular inventory rather than immediately as disposal material.

Sri Lanka’s textile and apparel sector is the project context. The reports do not claim an industry-wide waste quantity or measured environmental impact; the system is a research prototype that requires validation with representative local data.

2. Literature survey

Textile waste and circular economy

Circular-economy research focuses on reuse, repair, remanufacturing and recycling to retain material value. The project uses this framing to connect material information with practical exchange pathways.

AI-based fabric classification and defect detection

Computer vision and transfer learning can classify visual textile characteristics and provide preliminary condition signals. The project uses EfficientNet-B3 for 16-class fabric classification and a MobileNetV2 pathway for whole-image Defect/No Defect analysis.

Sustainability assessment

Sustainability outputs are model estimates that depend on material, process, baseline, units and system boundaries. They must not be presented as independent environmental certification.

Pricing, recommendation and marketplace analytics

Structured machine-learning models can support reference pricing, buyer-category ranking, listing-success prediction, sales-time estimation, demand classification and market-trend classification. The project evaluates these as decision-support outputs.

Research gap

The project opportunity is to coordinate material intelligence, sustainability interpretation, commercial matching, traceability and marketplace prediction around one textile listing, with shared identifiers and evidence limits. It does not claim that no similar system exists.

3. Research problem

Textile-waste suppliers and downstream users lack a reliable, consistent and decision-supportive digital process for identifying materials, assessing reuse potential, estimating commercial value and matching suitable buyers.

  • Material identity and visible condition can be inconsistent.
  • Environmental indicators depend on assumptions and boundaries.
  • Price and buyer suitability vary by quantity, location, condition and demand.
  • Marketplace events and model outputs need shared identifiers and traceability.

4. Research gap

Individual solutions may address classification, sustainability, pricing, recommendation or marketplace analytics in isolation. The project integrates these capabilities into one Sri Lankan-focused workflow while distinguishing model estimates from confirmed transaction facts.

5. Research question

How can an integrated AI-powered digital marketplace support textile-waste identification, sustainability assessment, pricing, buyer matching and marketplace decisions in Sri Lanka?

6. Research objectives

Main objective: Design, implement and evaluate an integrated AI-powered digital marketplace for Sri Lankan textile waste.

  • Identify and classify textile waste using AI-based image analysis.
  • Assess textile materials using sustainability and environmental indicators.
  • Estimate textile waste prices using machine learning.
  • Recommend suitable buyer-seller matches using intelligent recommendations.
  • Integrate the components into a digital marketplace and evaluate its potential impact.

7. Research methodology

  1. Problem and literature analysis

    Define the integrated problem, component boundaries and research gap.

  2. Data preparation

    Prepare image, structured sustainability and marketplace development data.

  3. Model development

    Train and evaluate component models using task-appropriate metrics.

  4. Integration

    Connect model services, backend workflows, shared fields and seller-facing outputs.

  5. Validation

    Test valid, invalid, failure and integration paths. Real-data and stakeholder validation remain future work.

8. Technologies used

Frontend

React, Vite, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, React Router and Lucide.

Backend

Node.js and Express.js for application and marketplace workflows.

ML service

Python and FastAPI for structured model inference.

Data layer

MongoDB/Mongoose are documented in the project stack and require repository verification.

Vision models

EfficientNet-B3 and MobileNetV2 pathway.

Tabular models

XGBoost regressors/classifiers and trained recommendation classifiers.
